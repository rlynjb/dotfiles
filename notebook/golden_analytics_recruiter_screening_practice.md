## 1. Tell me about yourself and your background.

I'm a software engineer with over eight years of experience at Switch, where I've mainly worked on front-end applications for enterprise customers. Over time, I've taken on more responsibility in application architecture, working with backend teams, and understanding systems beyond the UI.

More recently, I've been expanding into full-stack development through my work on our Facility Management application and through personal projects. I've also been exploring AI engineering in my own time, especially how to build applications around AI agents and tools.

## 2. What are you currently working on at Switch?

I'm currently working across both newer and legacy applications at Switch. One of our main initiatives is LDC Evo, a monorepo that modernizes functionality from older applications like LDC 1.0 and Customer Portal. I'm developing features for Facility Management, one of the applications within LDC Evo. That's also where I've been expanding my understanding of backend services and how data flows through the system end to end.

I also maintain existing applications, including Customer Portal and Security Audit Access, which our security team uses for quarterly badge access audits.

I've been at Switch since 2017 and was part of the original team that built some of these older applications. Because of that, I have a lot of historical context about how they work and why certain decisions were made. Newer developers and other teammates often come to me when they need help understanding those systems.

## 3. Why are you interested in Golden Analytics?

What interests me is the combination of full-stack product development and AI. I've spent most of my career building enterprise applications, and I want to keep growing into work where I can contribute across more of the stack.

The small engineering team also appeals to me because I'd have the chance to take ownership, learn quickly, and work closely with other engineers. I'm particularly interested in how the team uses AI tools in its development process.

## 4. What interests you about moving into full-stack and AI engineering?

After working primarily on the front end for years, I've become more curious about what happens beyond the UI—how services work, how data moves through a system, and how everything fits together.

That led me to explore backend development and then AI through self-study and projects like Buffr. I'm not trying to switch careers overnight; I'm building on my software engineering background and gradually expanding what I can design and build.

## 5. Have you worked with AI or LLMs professionally?

Not in my professional role so far. My AI experience has mainly come from self-study, personal projects, and a hackathon.

For example, I've been building Buffr, a project where I broke a research-and-recommendation workflow into modules and worked on the runtime and conditions that coordinate them. I also built LoomiConnect during a hackathon, where I worked with real analytics data and an MCP server. Those projects have helped me learn by building, but I wouldn't present them as production AI experience.

## 6. How much of your day-to-day work is coding versus collaboration and planning?

My current role involves more than writing code. A lot of my time goes into understanding requirements, collaborating with engineers and stakeholders, discussing technical approaches, reviewing work, and coordinating how features fit together.

The balance varies depending on the project and phase. I still stay close to implementation, and I build outside of work as well, especially when I want more hands-on experience with backend or AI technologies.

**Review note:** You've previously mentioned an estimate of about **5% coding / 95% other work** in a recruiter conversation. If that's still accurate, you can state it, but explain the timeframe and what the other 95% actually includes. Be ready to discuss your recent hands-on coding in detail; this role is likely to be implementation-heavy.

## 7. Are you comfortable with a small startup and four days a week in Bellevue?

I'm based in Seattle, so the Bellevue location is in the area I'm considering. I'm interested in a smaller team because I like the idea of having more ownership and understanding how different parts of a product fit together.

I understand the role is in office four days a week, and I'd be interested in learning more about the team's working style and expectations.

**Review note:** Add a direct **yes** only if you're genuinely comfortable with the four-day in-office schedule. If not, say what arrangement you could commit to rather than implying agreement.

## 8. What are your salary expectations?

I saw the posted base salary range of **$150,000 to $250,000**, plus equity. I'm interested in understanding the level, responsibilities, and overall compensation package before settling on a specific number. I'm open to discussing a competitive offer within the posted range.
